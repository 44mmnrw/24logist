# Visual style derived from the LogistRu reference set

Use these patterns to select a composition, not as a template that must appear in every image.

## Editorial hero

Best for announcements, release dates, registry status, product access, and offers.

- Start with a white or cool-white edge-to-edge canvas.
- Make one element unmistakably dominant: a date, percentage, duration, status, or short headline.
- Pair it with one substantial logistics/document illustration, usually on the right or lower edge.
- Keep the logo prominent in the upper area with clear space.
- Use faint flowing route lines, nodes, document cards, and small blue/green squares at the perimeter to create motion without reducing contrast.
- Separate a small category label with a thin green line or node when useful. Avoid adding extra UI panels merely to fill whitespace.

## Product-news card

For a feature release or service update:

- Use a compact blue date block in the upper-left when a date is part of the message.
- Keep explanatory copy in a clean left column and a connected document/cloud/truck visual in the right column.
- A short, large conclusion may anchor the bottom edge.
- Treat the whitespace between text and illustration as structural. Do not fill it with badges.

## Process diagram

- Place the initiating document or actor at the top and the completed document at the bottom or right.
- Use white cards with thin blue borders, navy labels, blue arrows, and green only for the final successful state.
- Keep all icons in one line style and align repeated nodes to a clear grid.
- A central hub is appropriate only when the process really converges on one entity such as a request card.

## Parallel scenario comparison

- Use a deep-navy header band with a short white title and a restrained route/process motif.
- Use equal columns and strong scenario numbering.
- Distinguish scenarios by semantic accents such as orange, teal, and blue while keeping body backgrounds light.
- Use a dark shared-benefit band and a high-contrast CTA footer only when both apply to every scenario.
- Density is acceptable when the hierarchy survives a 320 px preview; otherwise split or stack the content.

## Anti-patterns

- A large floating rounded container around the entire square composition.
- A small headline plus several generic cards that compete equally for attention.
- Fixed-width pills containing long Russian words.
- Redundant labels such as “Важная новость”, “Подтверждено”, and a headline that already communicates both.
- Decorative arrows that look like process arrows.
- Important content pushed against the right edge or hidden beneath an illustration.
- A plain empty canvas when subtle edge motifs would support the established visual language, unless the user explicitly asks to remove decoration.
